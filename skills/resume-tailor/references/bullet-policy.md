# Bullet Policy

## Product-evidence shape

Use the structure best supported by the evidence:

- `Action → product decision or deliverable → method → outcome`
- `Problem or evidence → prioritization decision → delivery or learning`
- `Outcome → product mechanism → verified scope`

Give each bullet one main product claim. Prefer concrete decisions, shipped capabilities, experiments, customer evidence, and business outcomes over generic traits.

## Ownership and wording

- Match the verb to actual ownership.
- Use JD vocabulary only when it accurately names existing work.
- Keep metrics attached to the action and scope that produced them.
- Do not replace meaningful evidence with keyword density.
- Put the strongest screening evidence early when natural.

## Length

Target one to two rendered lines per achievement bullet and never exceed two lines when the chosen layout supports that constraint. If a bullet overflows, remove lower-value explanation, compress wording, or revert a weak tailoring change. Never shrink font size, margins, or spacing to force a fit.

## Bold

Use zero or one bold span per experience bullet. Bold only the key product decision, capability, framework, or formal deliverable—never the action verb, tool, team count, or metric. Keep the span short, usually 3–8 words, and place it early only when natural.

Use no inline bold in project or portfolio bullets unless the uploaded template already requires it. Read all bold spans alone: they should form a concrete, credible, non-repetitive product-evidence chain.

## Change log

Log only material choices: evidence addition/replacement, bullet split/merge/removal, ownership/scope/causality correction, structural trade, or unresolved Core evidence. Do not log routine keywords, grammar, punctuation, skills order, compression, or bold styling.
